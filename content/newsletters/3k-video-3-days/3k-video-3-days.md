**Subject:** The $3K video I finished in 3 days

**Preview:** Harder to create does not equal better to watch.

---

Last week I finished a $3K video in 3 days.

Friday to Monday. Done.

And the part that surprised me most: it wasn't rushed. The client was happy. The video was genuinely good.

Because two years ago, I edited a video for the same client.

---

One scene required a map animation. Simple concept.

I built it manually in After Effects. 3D layers, a 3D camera, a custom Illustrator file of New York City.

It took me an entire day. For a 10-second clip.

The recent version? Found a simple web app. Done in 2 hours. And it actually looked better.

---

Harder to create does not equal better output.

There's your input (the work you do), your output (the finished video), and your outcome (a video that's actually great to watch). Most editors optimize for input. They measure success by how hard they worked.

Here's what changed for me.

---

Step 1: Ask Perplexity before you start building

Before you open After Effects, run the problem by Perplexity. Not "I need a map animation." Give it full context: what the scene is, what the narrator is saying, what you're trying to show the viewer.

That's how I found Avomap. That's how I find dashboard screenshots instead of building custom graphics. App interfaces instead of recreating them from scratch.

Find the actual thing. Don't build a visualization of it.

Step 2: Screenshot first, build second

For every graphic you're tempted to build: does it already exist somewhere?

Company dashboards. Google Trends. App interfaces. Website landing pages.

Most of the time, you can just screenshot it. Drop it in the timeline. Done.

This is Story Proof™. The viewer doesn't need a fancy visualization. They need to see the actual thing.

Step 3: Simplify your stylization

Create a stylization template. Lock it in. Reuse it.

If one client revision means touching 15 layers, it's too complex.

Step 4: Find all your music at once

Do a rough cut first (V0). Then open Epidemic with 2 filters max: one mood, maybe one genre. Download everything that fits the overall vibe. Don't try to match songs to specific scenes yet.

Aim for 5-6 tracks, then use "similar tracks" to fill the gaps.

btw, don't be afraid to reuse the same track across different videos for the same client. They won't notice.

Step 5: One animation per sentence

Never create more than one animation per sentence.

Identify the one idea the line is trying to convey. Show it in the simplest way possible. One icon. One graphic.

More animations per sentence means harder to process, which means a worse video.

---

Stop measuring how long the video took to make.
Start measuring how good it is to watch.

---

I wasn't just finishing faster. I was finishing with more headspace left over. Headspace to think about the story, the pacing, the decisions that actually separate a $500 edit from a $3K one.
