# Newsletter: Price Last™

**Subject:** I've never taken a sales call

**Preview:** here's how I close clients anyway

---

In my entire time as a video editor, I never got on a single call to close a client.

Every client. Closed over DM.

And I'm not special at sales. I just figured out the order matters more than anything else.

Here's what most editors do:

Client reaches out.

Editor drops the price.

Client ghosts.

And the editor thinks it's a price problem...

It's not. It's a sequencing problem.

The client didn't ghost because your rate was too high. They ghosted because you gave them the number before they felt understood.

Here's the thing:

Every creator who reaches out thinks their situation is completely unique. Their channel, their audience, their vision. And they need to feel like what they're paying for was built specifically for them.

Drop the price before they feel that — you're just another number in their DMs.

I call it Price Last™: understand everything first, price comes last.

Here's the exact process. All of it happens in under 10 messages:

*[image: Price Last™ Framework slide — all 6 steps]*

1) Connect personally.

Get off X or email as fast as possible. Move to WhatsApp or Telegram. One qualifying question — how many videos per month are you looking to post — is usually enough to earn that.

2) Do the research.

Before you ask a single question, look at their channel. You can find almost everything about their current situation yourself. Every question you skip is one less reason to ghost you.

3) Ask about their vision.

How many videos per month? What style? Any creators they look up to as a reference?

Two questions max. You know the current state from your research. You just need the destination.

4) Spot the pain.

One question: "Are you working with an editor right now?"

That answer tells you everything. If they are, and uploads are still inconsistent — you know what's wrong. If they're editing themselves — they're burning 15+ hours per video on top of everything else.

5) Bridge the gap.

One message that proves you were actually paying attention.

You understand where they are. You understand where they want to go. Show them you get it — not with a pitch, not with a portfolio dump.

Make sure they understand that you understand.

6) Drop the price.

Now you say the number. Tie it to their specific situation — the video count, the style, the schedule they need.

Don't justify it. Don't soften it. Say it and let it land.

---

None of this is complicated.

But most editors skip it because they're too eager to get to the number.

Same thing happens in the edit — jumping to effects before knowing what the video actually needs.

Button pusher behavior in the DMs. Button pusher behavior in the timeline.

Same mistake.

The editors who close consistently aren't better salespeople. They're better listeners.

They let the client talk, understand the situation, and price becomes the natural conclusion of a conversation that's already been won.
