## THE 3-LEVEL SYSTEM

The bigger brands are starting to pour serious money into organic content. And so the bar to win on social in 2026 is higher than it's ever been.

But here's what most brands get wrong. They see what the big players are doing — cinematic shoots, full production crews, agency retainers — and they think that's where they need to start.

It's not.

There are three levels to the social playbook. Beginner, intermediate, and advanced. Each level requires different operations, different bandwidth, and a different team skill set. Over the next few weeks, I'll cover all three. Today we're starting with Level 1 — the foundation.

And don't skip over this next part. Because the reality is, it's very easy to get excited, aim too high with your content strategy, and never take off.

---

## HOW TO KNOW YOUR LEVEL

After working with brands at every level from 7 to 10 figures, I've seen it all. Brands with incredible operations but the wrong team for their content strategy. Brands with a large bandwidth and the right skill set, but no operations — so they can't scale.

It comes down to three elements. Operations. Bandwidth. Team skill set. And all three need to work together.

Here's my advice: Execute optimistically. Strategize pessimistically.

Don't build your strategy assuming everything goes right. It never does. One or two small hurdles will implode a content plan that was designed for perfect conditions.

But always punch above your belt when you execute. Use every piece of content as a way to test your ops, stretch your bandwidth, and improve your team's skill set.

Ask yourself three questions:

One — do my operations break if I double the content volume in the next 30 days?

Two — does the quality of my content drop if I double the content volume in the next 30 days?

Three — does my team's skill set match the content I aim to produce in the next 30 days?

If you nail two out of three, you can move to the next level and use the next 30 days to close the gap. If you're hitting one out of three, things will break. Spend the next 30 to 60 days building your infrastructure before you level up.

For most brands watching this — you're at Level 1. And that's not a weakness. That's your starting line.

## FORMAT 1: ONE-SHOT VIDEOS

The first format is one-shot videos. Sometimes called vignettes. One continuous clip. One moment. One idea. And they work at every stage of the content funnel — awareness, engagement, conversion.

There are three levels to the execution.

**Level 1 is a single scene video with a text overlay. Lo-fi.**

The formula is simple. A title hook — that's the text on screen — plus a visual that brings it to life. Text hook. Visual hook. That's it.

Look at what Heart and Soil does. Single clip. Compelling text. The visual sells the vibe. Zero production budget.

The best practice here is making sure the text and the visual work together, not against each other. The text creates curiosity. The visual satisfies it.

**Level 2 is the same concept, but hi-fi.**

The difference — you layer in a constant action that works in tandem with the text. Movement keeps the viewer locked in while the text delivers the message.

tomc_fit does this well. The visual has energy and motion. The text rides on top. It's a simple upgrade, but the retention difference is real.

**Level 3 is the vignette.**

These are short moments that transport the viewer into your brand's world. And the best vignettes are made through the over-optimization of one of three things: the angle, the aesthetic, or the action.

another_adventure_gram is a strong example. It's not about expensive gear. It's about being intentional with how you capture the moment. One shot. One emotion. One world.

The beauty of one-shots: the ceiling is high, but the floor is free. You can start at Level 1 today with your phone and a text overlay app. Then level up as your skill set improves.

## FORMAT 2: TEN-SHOT VIDEOS

The second format is ten-shot videos. The concept — you tell a quick, snappy story through a mix of various fast shots. Usually around ten of them, cut together to form a narrative.

This format is powerful because the pacing does the heavy lifting. The viewer doesn't have time to get bored. Each new shot re-engages their attention.

There are two levels.

**Level 1 is ten shots with text overlay.**

The title hook provides clarity and context while opening a curiosity gap. Then the visuals close that gap shot by shot.

FUNBOY is a good example of this. The text sets up the question. The ten shots answer it. Fast. Punchy. Satisfying.

**Level 2 is ten shots with narration.**

Instead of text doing the work, you write a script and match specific shots to specific lines. This takes more planning — you need a shot list that maps segments of your script to each shot — but the payoff is a more polished, story-driven piece of content.

BlankedStudios does this well. When the narration and the visuals sync up, the content feels cinematic. Without the cinematic budget.

The key with ten-shot videos: shot variety. Close-ups, wide shots, detail shots, action shots. The mix creates rhythm. Rhythm creates retention.

## FORMAT 3: CAROUSELS

The third format is carousels. And I think these are the most underrated content type at Level 1.

Three reasons every brand should leverage them.

One — they're the best format for your existing followers. People who already follow you are more likely to swipe through a carousel. It's content designed for the audience you've already built.

Two — they're great for re-packaging. Take content that already performed well — a video, a tweet, a blog post — and package it as a carousel. The idea is proven. You're just changing the delivery.

Three — they can go nuclear. Carousels get pushed into the Reels algorithm. That means they can reach people well beyond your current following.

Here are five ways to use them.

**Recreate a weekly vlog.** Puresport takes their weekly moments — events, behind the scenes, product interactions — and turns them into a carousel. It feels authentic. Personal. Costs nothing.

**Campaign production behind the scenes.** Fern documents their campaign shoots through carousels. It builds credibility because you're showing the craft behind the brand.

**Category entertainment or education.** Graffeocoffee creates carousels like "What Your Cafe Order Says About You." It's entertaining, it's shareable, and it positions them as the authority in their space.

**ASMR.** Fern also does ASMR carousels. Atmospheric. Sensory. Beautiful. These transport the viewer into the brand world without saying a single word.

**The Journey.** Nude Project documents their entire origin story through carousels. "From the local post office" to "this was our first warehouse" to "and somehow... this is where we are now." It's powerful because it invites the audience into the story of how the brand was built.

Carousels require zero video skill. Just photos, intention, and a story to tell.

## FORMAT 4: HOW TO / WHY WE DO IT

The fourth format is something I call "How To / Why We Do It" content. And this one ties everything together.

The concept — you're explaining how you do something or why you do something. But the angle is what matters. You're not just educating. You're building credibility around what makes you different within your category.

Here's the best practice. Collect your top comments. Collect the FAQ. Look at the comments on your competitors' content. Use a tool like Ask The Public to find the top questions in your category. Then answer those questions — but include your positioning, your differentiation, your unique experience, or your contrarian take.

Brownie God is a perfect example. "Why bakeries don't use chocolate bars." It's educational. It positions them as the expert. And it differentiates their product. All in one piece of content.

This format works because it answers the questions people are already asking, but through the lens of your brand. That's what turns a generic answer into a trust-building moment.
