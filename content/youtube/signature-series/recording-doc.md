## WHAT A SIGNATURE SERIES IS (AND ISN'T)

- Not the same as a social show — a social show lives on a separate account as its own IP
- A Signature Series lives on your main page, scaled across multiple episodes
- It's a pillar within your content strategy, not a separate strategy
- More budget-friendly than a social show — doesn't require all the steps
- Still a massive growth lever

> "Think of it like this... a social show is a spin-off on another channel. A Signature Series is a recurring segment on your main channel that people come back for."

> "And there's a specific framework for building one that actually works. Three elements."

## ELEMENT 1: REPEATABLE CONCEPT

- Going into 2026, the most important thing you can develop as a brand is familiarity
- A repeatable concept earns recognition — people see it and know it's you before they even read your name
- The concept needs to scale infinitely with minimal friction
- If it requires too much time, too much coordination, too much production — it will die

> "And I've seen this play out in real time."

**Matt Choi — The Mile Challenge**
- Every contestant sets a goal mile time, Matt challenges them to hit it
- Going on two years strong
- Requires 15 minutes and one videographer on a scooter with an iPhone
- That's it. Fifteen minutes. An iPhone. And it's been running for two years.

> "But here's the other side of that coin..."

**Matt Choi — The failed series**
- He had a second series — run 3 miles with someone, interview them, cut it into a short-form clip
- Killed it. Too much friction.
- A full hour of filming, then cutting an hour of footage into a 45-second to 90-second clip
- That friction led to ditching the idea and doubling down on the Mile Challenge

> "So the question isn't 'what's a cool idea?' The question is 'what can you produce infinitely without burning out?'"

**Onda Bar — Ticket To Table**
- Any cocktail, appetizer, entree, or dessert becomes an episode
- Scalable operations — you can build a system around this
- Minimal friction — you're documenting what already occurs
- 2 million views

> "Repeatable. Scalable. Frictionless. That's element one. But a repeatable concept alone isn't enough — because someone else can copy it."

---

## ELEMENT 2: UNIQUE TO YOU

- The easiest way to win a race is to be the only one running it
- You need to conceptualize a series that is one of one because of the uniqueness factor applied to it
- Identify the elements of your series that nobody else can replicate
- Then lock those in

> "Let me show you what this looks like."

**Nitro Bar — "What Would You Make Me If?"**
- Four elements make the series impossible to copy:
  - The Characters
  - The Menu
  - The Scenario
  - The Location
- These are the pieces others can't replicate
- It's vital to establish key pieces that are distinct and marked with your brand

> "That way, the only one showing up to the track on day 1 and day 90... is you."

> "So you have a repeatable concept that's unique to you. But there's one more element that turns a good series into a bingeable one."

---

## ELEMENT 3: CHARACTER DRIVEN

- Characters are the vehicle of value
- You don't only want familiarity around the series — you want affinity around the characters
- People come back for people, not formats

**Budget tiers:**
- Tighter budget — your characters are your staff, customers, or employees
- Bigger budget — factor in creators, influencers, or actors

> "Either way, the character needs to be the centerpiece."

**Yes Chef Supply — The Off Menu**
- Different chefs creating different dishes while they tell their story
- The chef is the centerpiece — everything is built around them
- Gives the viewer a reason to feel connected
- Each episode is a new chef, a new story, but the same format — that's what makes it bingeable

> "Repeatable concept. Unique to you. Character driven. That's the framework."
