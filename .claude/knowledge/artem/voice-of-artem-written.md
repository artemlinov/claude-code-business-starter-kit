# VOICE OF ARTEM — WRITTEN CONTENT

Written-content voice patterns for Full-Time Editor: emails, newsletters, and Skool community posts.

This file extends the core `voice-of-artem.md` with patterns specific to written platforms. Load both files when writing any written content.

---

## The Written Voice in 30 Words

Artem's writing reads like he's thinking out loud and you get to overhear. Direct but warm. Short lines. Generous spacing. One clear ask per piece. No fluff, no hype.

---

## How Written Platforms Compare

| Element | Email | Newsletter | Skool |
|---------|-------|------------|-------|
| Tone | Intimate, 1-on-1 | Most intimate, story-driven | Community, casual |
| Paragraphs | 1-3 sentences | Full stories, but short paragraphs | 1-2 sentences max |
| Lists | Line breaks only (no bullets) | `–` lists or numbered `1)` format | Emoji numbers (1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣) |
| CTAs | One per email, arrow (→) | Bar + Teaser close, PS section | Engagement prompts (comments > clicks) |
| Sign-off | "Talk [day]." / "Artem" | "See you there," / "– Artem" | End with engagement prompt |
| Bold | Never | Never | Never |
| Personality | Like a text from a friend | Like coffee with a friend | Like talking to friends in a room |

## Shared Written Rules

These apply to ALL written content — email, newsletter, and Skool.

### Language

- Contractions always: "gonna" > "going to", "wanna" > "want to", "it's" > "it is"
- Use "quick chat" not "brief call", "straight up" not "honestly"
- Colons before lists and explanations (not periods)
- Ellipsis (...) for trailing thoughts — 1-2 per piece max
- Double question marks ("??") for rhetorical emphasis — on brand
- "lol", "btw" — fine in newsletters and Skool, sparingly in email

### Formatting

- NO bold text in body copy (ever, on any platform)
- NO emojis in body text (except emoji numbers for Skool lists)
- NO walls of text — every line must breathe
- One thought per paragraph
- One sentence per line when possible
- Generous whitespace everywhere

### Tone

- NO hype or superlatives ("amazing", "incredible", "life-changing")
- NO artificial urgency (unless true)
- NO guilt-tripping
- NO guru energy
- NO corporate openers ("I hope this email finds you well")
- NO "just checking in"
- Direct, warm, thinking out loud

### Reframes That Convert

These work across all written platforms:

**"What has actually changed?"** — Creates awareness of stuckness without being preachy.
```
What has actually changed in the past 6 months?

Not what you've tried. Not what you've learned. What has actually changed in your income, your clients, your situation?
```

**"What happens if nothing changes?"** — Urgency through cost of inaction, not pressure.
```
If you're in the exact same position 6 months from now. Same income. Same clients. Same frustrations.

→ What does that actually cost you?
```

**"Difficult questions = real progress"**
```
I'm not asking this to pressure you.

I'm asking because difficult questions are where the real progress is made.
```

**"I'd rather you not join"** — With-or-without-you energy.
```
If it's not a fit, I'll tell you straight up. I'd rather you not join than join, spend your money, and not get the best results possible.
```

## Email-Specific Rules

### Greetings

**First email in a sequence:** `Hey {{name}},`

**Follow-up emails:** `{{name}},` (no "Hey")

### Paragraphs

- 1-3 sentences max per paragraph
- One thought per paragraph

### Lists

- 3-5 items max
- NO bullet points or dashes — just line breaks

```
✓ Correct:

What did you make last month? Last 3 months?
What are you charging per video?
How many clients do you have?

✗ Incorrect:

- What did you make last month/last 3 months?
- What are you charging per video?
- How many clients do you have?
```

### Emphasis

- Use `→` before the primary CTA or key question (ONE per email max)
- Use line breaks and spacing for emphasis instead of bold

```
→ Watch it here.

→ If you haven't watched the video I sent yet, watch it now.

→ What does that actually cost you?
```

### Sign-Offs

| Sign-off | When to use |
|----------|-------------|
| "Talk [day of week]." | Sequences — use the day the next email sends |
| "Looking forward to it." | Warm, first email, anticipation |
| "See you tomorrow." | Direct, reminder emails |
| "Talk soon." | Standalone emails only (not sequences) |
| "See you in 60." | Ultra-short, 1-hour reminders |

Always just "Artem" (no dash, no hyphen, no "- Artem")

### Subject Line Patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Direct question | "quick question before our call" |
| Specific number | "the $2K/video question" |
| Curiosity gap | "one thing I forgot to mention" |
| Time-based | "12 hours from now" |
| Pattern interrupt | "this might sound weird" |
| Continuation | "re: your call tomorrow" |

Rules: 4-8 words, lowercase, no periods, no emojis, personal not promotional.

### Preview Text

- 40-90 characters
- Complements subject line (doesn't repeat it)
- Often a fragment, not a complete sentence

| Subject | Preview |
|---------|---------|
| "just booked — quick video inside" | "3 minutes, worth watching before we talk" |
| "12 hours from now" | "one question to sit with before we talk" |
| "no pressure, just curious" | "noticed you went quiet" |

### Email Structure

**One Ask Per Email** — every email has ONE thing they need to do. Never stack CTAs.

**The Thinking Out Loud Pattern:**
1. State something direct
2. Add context or a question
3. Answer or reframe
4. Connect to the reader
5. Clear ask

### Email Templates

**Pre-Call Confirmation (First Email):**
```
Hey {{contact.first_name}},

You just booked a [call type] with me.

Here's the deal:

[What the call is / what to expect]

[What you're NOT going to do — remove fear]

Before we talk, [pre-work request with clear value].

→ [Single CTA]

Your call:

{{appointment.start_time}}
{{appointment.meeting_location}}

Looking forward to it.

Artem
```

**Reminder (Day Before):**
```
{{contact.first_name}},

We're talking tomorrow.

Just a heads up: [what to expect / prepare for]

[2-3 specific questions you'll ask — builds anticipation]

The more honest you are, the more useful this call will be for both of us.

→ [Pre-work CTA if applicable]

Your call:

{{appointment.start_time}}
{{appointment.meeting_location}}

See you tomorrow.

Artem
```

**Reminder (Same Day):**
```
{{contact.first_name}},

Our call is in [X hours].

[Single reframe or question to prime them]

Come ready to [specific mindset instruction].

Your call:

{{appointment.start_time}}
{{appointment.meeting_location}}

Talk soon.

Artem
```

**Post-Call Follow-Up:**
```
{{contact.first_name}},

Good talking to you.

[Reference something specific from the call — proves you listened]

[Restate the key insight or decision point from the call]

→ [Clear next step]

[Timeframe or what happens next]

Artem
```

**Reactivation:**
```
{{contact.first_name}},

I noticed [specific observation — they went quiet, didn't book, etc.].

No pressure. Just curious what happened.

[2-3 possible reasons — shows empathy, not accusation]

I'm not trying to sell you on anything. I'm genuinely asking because [honest reason].

→ Hit reply and let me know where you're at.

Either way, [genuine well-wish].

Artem
```

### Email Rhythm Patterns

**Break sentences into standalone lines.** Dense compound sentences kill the rhythm.
```
✗ Not because it helps the video. Because you're scared of empty space.
✓ Not because it helps the video. But because you're scared of empty space.
```

**Conversational interjections as transitions:**
```
The second type though?

Solid, right??

And here's the craziest part:
```

**Numbered lists for structured arguments:** Use `1)` `2)` `3)` format with explanation as separate paragraphs underneath.
```
1) No creative direction skills.

Editors execute, creative directors think for the viewer.

2) Bad positioning.

You look like every other editor.

Same portfolio.
Same outreach.
```

**Never echo subject/preview in the opening.** Jump straight into new content.

**Shorter is always better.** Remove setup sentences, transition phrases, anything the reader can infer, repeated conclusions.

### Sequence Writing Patterns

- Use concrete numbers, not ranges ($1,500/video not $1,000+/video)
- "Same skills" not "same skill set" — simpler flows better
- Reference niches carefully — don't say "YouTube" when the audience IS YouTube editors
- Name programs fully when introducing price ("Full-Time Editor is $3K" not "The full program is $3K")
- Trust the sequence — don't repeat details covered in earlier emails
- Prefer landing pages over reply CTAs when a page exists

## Newsletter-Specific Rules

### Structure

Every edition follows this flow:

1. **Hook** — Personal story or contrarian statement
2. **Story** — The journey, the struggle, the insight
3. **Framework** — The actionable takeaway
4. **Bar** — Memorable closing line
5. **PS Section** — CTAs (2nd most-read part after hook)

### Step Format

Framework steps are written as prose with a plain heading — never bold numbered bullets.

```
Step 1: Connect personally

Get off X or email as fast as possible.

Ideally, to WhatsApp or Telegram. Or whatever they use for work: Slack, Notion, ClickUp.

But be careful – you're just a stranger on the internet. For them to connect with you personally...

They need to know you might be useful to them.
```

Sub-items within a step use `–` lists:
```
Identify:

– How many videos they're publishing rn
– What genuinely can be improved in their videos
– Anything to complement them on, like camera presence or audio quality
```

### The Bar + Teaser Close

Two parts. First the Bar — a quotable one-liner that crystallizes the whole lesson. Then a Teaser — a NEW curiosity hook that creates desire to click the CTA.

```
[The Bar]
Stop thinking about how to close the client.
Start thinking about how to help them.

[The Teaser — introduces something new, doesn't just restate]
But you know what's the craziest thing?

I actually don't think you "close" or "land" $5K/month clients.

For those type of clients, you need to know how to build relationships.

And you might even be able to build it with one of the clients you have already.

[Natural CTA lead-in]
I cover exactly how to do this in the Quality-First Clients™ pillar of the Creative Director Shift™.

You can watch the whole thing for free right now inside my Skool community.

See you there,

– Artem
```

### Engagement P.S.

The default P.S. for broadcast newsletters is a reply-invite, not a sales pitch:
```
P.S. Are you closing clients on calls or over DMs? Hit reply and let me know. I read every single one.
```

### Newsletter CTA Structure

**PS Section:**
```
PS. If you want my personal help scaling to $5K/month, apply here: [landing page link]

PPS. If you're not ready for that but want to connect with other editors, join my free community here: [Skool link]
```

### CTA Sign-Off

```
✓ See you there,
✓ See you inside,

✗ Grab it inside the free community here:
```

### Casual Language

"lol", "btw", trailing "..." — all fine in newsletters. More so than any other platform.

```
How do we do it?

By looking at their channel lol.
```

## Skool-Specific Rules

### Titles

- Short and punchy
- Can use ellipsis for curiosity ("Something new is dropping Feb 14...")
- Lowercase unless proper noun
- No clickbait

### Paragraphs

- 1-2 sentences max (even shorter than email)
- One thought per line
- Generous whitespace

### Lists

- Emoji numbers (1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣ 4️⃣ 5️⃣) for structured lists — THE key difference from email
- One item per line
- Keep list items short

### Emphasis

- Arrow (→) before key info or CTA — ONE per post max
- NO bold, NO bullet points with dashes

### Engagement Prompts

Every post ends with an invitation to engage:
- "Drop a comment."
- "Reply if you have questions."
- "I wanna know who's paying attention."

These replace email-style CTAs. Skool is about community interaction.

### Skool-Specific Patterns

- More vulnerability than email — share nervousness, uncertainty, behind-the-scenes feelings
- "People in this room" / "people in this community" — creates belonging
- Can reference existing members directly

### Post Types

**Announcement / Tease:**
```
Title: Something new is dropping Feb 14...

I've been building something I've never done before.

And I'm kinda nervous about it.

It's for a very specific type of editor:

1️⃣ Someone who's in this community
2️⃣ Someone who knows they're capable of more, but feels stuck in the same spot.

[continue with tease, end with engagement prompt]
```

**Launch / Offer:**
```
Title: [Offer name] is live.

[Reference previous tease post]

[One-line description of the offer]

[Key details: price, spots, deadline]

Here's what you get:

1️⃣ [Item]
2️⃣ [Item]
3️⃣ [Item]

[After details + no lock-in note]

[Link]

[Disclaimer for existing members if applicable]

Reply if you have questions.
```

**Disclaimers for existing members:**

"If you're already inside Full-Time Editor — you're my priority. Nothing changes for you. This is for editors who aren't ready for the full program yet."

### What to Avoid in Skool

- NO walls of text — posts get skipped in feeds
- NO bullet points with dashes (use emoji numbers)
- NO multiple links — one link per post max
